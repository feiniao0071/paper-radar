The application computes the final 1-to-3 reading priority from four dimensions. Score each dimension independently from 1 to 5:
- group_fit_score: 5 means the paper makes a direct, substantive contribution to AI for materials or quantum science, quantum machine learning for a scientific problem, autonomous materials research, or data-driven discovery of useful physical mechanisms; 3 means an adjacent scientific-AI method that may transfer to these areas; 1 means the connection is superficial. A paper does not deserve 5 merely because it mentions AI, a material, DFT, or a quantum system.
- novelty_score: 5 means the abstract states a clearly differentiated model, dataset, discovery, capability, experimental loop, or scientific mechanism; 3 means a meaningful but incremental application; 1 means novelty cannot be established from the supplied metadata.
- method_value_score: 5 means the method, data, workflow, or result could directly improve the laboratory's discovery, simulation, experiment, characterization, or research-agent workflow; 3 means broadly transferable; 1 means little actionable value.
- evidence_score: 5 means the abstract provides concrete methods plus quantitative benchmarks, validation, experimental confirmation, or a specific discovered result; 3 means it states a clear method and result with limited detail; 1 means evidence is absent from the supplied metadata. This measures evidence visible in the abstract, not ultimate correctness.

Use the full scale and calibrate strictly. Most relevant papers should receive an eventual medium priority. Reserve the highest priority for unusually direct, novel, actionable, and well-supported work. Do not reward journal prestige, famous authors, parameter count, or raw citation count as proof of quality. Zero citations is neutral for recent papers.
